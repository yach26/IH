# AgroTwin AI — Demo / Judging Presentation Script

*Target length: 8-10 minutes talk + live demo, then Q&A. Each section below
is a slide + speaking notes. Adjust pacing to the time you're actually given.*

---

## Slide 1 — Title

**Say:**
> "AgroTwin AI — a Digital Krishi Twin for sustainable fertilizer decisions.
> Piloted for farmers in Kolhapur and Jalgaon, Maharashtra, but built to
> work correctly for any farmer, anywhere."

---

## Slide 2 — The problem

**Say:**
> "Indian farmers over- or under-apply fertilizer constantly — not because
> they don't care, but because they don't have field-specific, timely
> guidance. Generic advice ('apply X bags per acre') ignores their actual
> soil test, their crop's actual growth stage, and this week's actual
> weather. Over-application wastes money and pollutes runoff.
> Under-application costs yield. And most digital tools in this space are
> either static calculators or black-box AI that can't explain itself —
> which agronomists and farmers rightly don't trust."

---

## Slide 3 — What we built

**Say:**
> "AgroTwin is a living digital twin of each field. It doesn't just output
> a number — every recommendation answers six questions: WHAT to apply, HOW
> MUCH, WHEN, WHY, based on WHAT evidence, and HOW CERTAIN we are. And
> critically: the fertilizer math is never generated by an LLM. It's a
> deterministic calculation, sourced from real ICAR/MPKV agronomy
> guidelines, that we can trace and audit line by line."

*(Show the six-question framework as a visual if you have slides —
this is the core differentiator, spend real time on it.)*

---

## Slide 4 — Architecture (keep this simple, don't read the whole stack)

**Say:**
> "A farmer's field data flows through five stages: Soil, Crop, and
> Weather agents gather the real inputs. A deterministic Nutrient Ledger
> computes exactly what's missing versus the sourced RDF target for that
> crop and stage. An Optimizer converts that gap into real fertilizer
> products — DAP, Urea, MOP. A Validation layer checks the plan against
> agronomic rules and current weather. And a Retrieval-Augmented Generation
> layer attaches real evidence citations — so every recommendation can be
> traced back to an actual government agronomy document, not a model's
> internal weights."

**If asked "so where's the AI?":**
> "The AI is doing evidence retrieval and optional narrative phrasing —
> never the fertilizer math itself. That's a deliberate trust decision:
> LLMs hallucinate, and a hallucinated fertilizer quantity is a real-world
> safety problem, not just an inconvenience."

## Slide 5 — LIVE DEMO 1: A new farmer, start to finish

*(Do this live, not in slides — it's your strongest moment.)*

**Script:**
1. Open the Upload page. "Here's a real soil health card photo." Upload it.
   → "The system read Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, pH, Organic Carbon
   and Electrical Conductivity automatically, each with a confidence score.
   Nothing is saved yet — the farmer reviews and confirms first."
2. Click Confirm. → "Now it's part of this field's real record."
3. Open the Farm dashboard for that field. Point at the **Official
   Recommendation** panel. "This is the hero of the page, deliberately —
   big numbers, not buried in a metrics grid. DAP, Urea, MOP, in kg/ha.
   Below it: numbered next steps, and a stamp-style confidence badge —
   just like an official government advisory notice."
4. Click **Listen**. Let it read a sentence or two aloud. "This reads the
   full field status — soil health, weather, the recommendation, next
   steps — in English, Hindi, or Marathi, for farmers who find a long
   technical screen hard to read."
5. Scroll to **Recommendation History**. "Every past recommendation is
   here — full audit trail. And an agronomist can override any plan with
   a documented reason —" *(click Override, fill in a quick example,
   submit)* "— and it updates the farmer's dashboard immediately, fully
   audited."

## Slide 8 — What we explicitly refused to fake

**Say (this builds real credibility with judges — be specific):**
> "Throughout building this, our rule was: if we don't have a real number,
> say so — never show a plausible-looking fake one. Concretely, we caught
> and removed: a hardcoded fallback location that leaked into every field
> without real GPS coordinates; a hardcoded 17mm weather default; a
> sugarcane-only soil-health formula that would have silently produced
> wrong scores for every other crop; and a What-If yield/cost projection
> that was labelled as an 'AI Yield Projection' but was actually a raw
> percentage multiplier — now honestly labelled a 'scenario estimate.'
> We treat missing data as a valid, real state a farmer might genuinely be
> in — not a bug to hide."

## Slide 9 — Tech stack (quick, judges usually want this listed)

**Say:**
> "FastAPI and SQLite/PostgreSQL on the backend, with SciPy for an
> optional linear-programming optimizer, XGBoost for a directional yield
> model trained on real Kolhapur soil and yield data, a hybrid BM25 +
> dense-embedding retrieval layer for evidence citations, and EasyOCR for
> reading soil health cards. Next.js and TypeScript on the frontend, with
> the browser's own text-to-speech API for multilingual voice output — no
> extra service needed for that."

---

## Slide 10 — What's next / honest roadmap

**Say:**
> "We're explicit about what's not done yet, because a team that hides its
> gaps hasn't actually understood its own system. Fertilizer cost is
> currently an engineering estimate — we haven't sourced a real price
> table yet. Voice is currently read-aloud only; voice *input* isn't built.
> The yield model covers four crops in two districts and honestly
> abstains everywhere else. And there's no multi-tenant authentication
> yet — appropriate for a pilot, not for public production. Every one of
> these is a scoped, known next step, not a surprise we're hoping you
> won't find."

## Anticipated judge questions — prepared answers

**"Why not just use an LLM for everything, it'd be faster to build?"**
> "We did use one — for evidence retrieval and optional narrative
> phrasing. Not for the fertilizer math, deliberately. An LLM outputting a
> confident-sounding but wrong kg/ha number is a real agronomic risk, not
> a UX nitpick. Determinism and traceability were a design requirement
> from day one, not an afterthought."

**"How do you know your RDF numbers are correct?"**
> "They're sourced directly from MPKV and ICAR published recommended-dose
> guidelines and the district-specific Jalgaon RDF document — cited by
> file name in every recommendation shown. We didn't derive or estimate
> them ourselves."

**"What happens if the soil test is wrong?"**
> "Two safeguards: OCR confidence scoring flags any value it's unsure of
> for farmer review before it's ever saved, and a stale-soil-data flag
> automatically lowers system confidence on old data. An agronomist can
> also override any plan with a documented reason at any time."

**"Does this scale beyond two districts?"**
> "Yes — we specifically tested this. The fertilizer math is keyed by
> crop and growth stage, not district; weather is keyed by the field's own
> GPS coordinates. We onboarded and fully verified a field in Pune,
> outside both pilot districts, end-to-end."

**"What was the hardest engineering problem?"**
> "Keeping the 'never invent a number' rule honest under real usage — it's
> easy to say, much harder to enforce when a UI naturally wants to show
> *something* rather than a blank state. Most of our hardening passes were
> specifically hunting for places a default value had silently substituted
> for missing data."
